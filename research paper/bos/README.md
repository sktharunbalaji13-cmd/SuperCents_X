# BOS — Break of Structure

## What it is (retail / Smart Money Concepts terminology)
A **Break of Structure (BOS)** is the continuation signal in ICT / SMC market-structure analysis. In an established trend, price makes a sequence of higher highs / higher lows (bullish) or lower highs / lower lows (bearish). A **bullish BOS** fires when price *closes above* the prior swing-high pivot; a **bearish BOS** when price *closes below* the prior swing-low pivot. Because it happens *in the direction of the prevailing trend*, a BOS is treated as a **continuation** event — the market "breaking structure" to keep trending. Detection usually requires: swing pivots, a confirmed close beyond the protected pivot, and (in stricter definitions) displacement/volume.

## Academic analog
BOS has **no formal peer-reviewed definition** (it is retail jargon). Its underlying mechanism is the **support/resistance level break** and **breakout / self-fulfilling-prophecy** behaviour studied in technical-analysis and market-microstructure research. The papers below describe the mathematics and statistical reality of price levels being broken.

## Papers in this folder
1. **2103.02331 — The Support and Resistance Line Method: An Analysis via Optimal Stopping (Henderson, Jacka, Liu, Maeda)**
   Formalises price bouncing between support/resistance "states of nature" and derives optimal buy/sell stopping rules — the quantitative backbone of break-and-retest behaviour.
2. **1401.1888 — Dynamical Models of Stock Prices Based on Technical Trading Rules Part I (L.-X. Wang)**
   Converts technical heuristics (incl. support/resistance and trend-line rules) into fuzzy dynamical systems that reproduce trending, jumps and self-fulfilling behaviour — exactly the dynamics SMC's "BOS continuation" assumes.
3. **1110.5197 — Memory effects in stock price dynamics: evidences of technical trading (Garzarelli, Cristelli, Zaccaria, Pietronero)**
   Empirical evidence that prices *re-bounce* more often than they *cross* support/resistance levels, and that breakouts leave detectable memory effects — a statistical basis for why BOS levels matter.
